\title{Residual Surrogates for Nonlinear DSGE Estimation:\\
An Auditable Target-Corrected Workflow}
\author{Anonymous working draft}
\date{October 23, 2026}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Repeated nonlinear solution can dominate likelihood-based estimation of dynamic
stochastic general equilibrium models. We develop an auditable workflow in
which a neural network learns the difference between extended-path trajectories
and a parameter-specific first-order solution. Cheap surrogate gradients form
Hamiltonian proposals; an endpoint Metropolis test preserves the density
actually evaluated there. This distinction matters because correcting for a
surrogate profile objective is not exact inference for the nonlinear model. On
the complete 23-equation Gal\'i model, preserved deterministic pilots solve 80
targets with maximum residual $9.92\times10^{-9}$, improve calibration-set
error over the first-order model by 53.9 percent, and match Python and JAX
evaluations within $1.71\times10^{-13}$. These results establish software
feasibility, not posterior fidelity or speed. The paper therefore couples the
method with frozen gates for stochastic-solution accuracy, independent parity,
untouched test performance, posterior agreement, multichain sampling, and
same-work timing.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Likelihood-based estimation of a nonlinear DSGE model repeats a costly
numerical experiment at every parameter proposal. A single evaluation may
require a steady state, a local solution, nonlinear conditional trajectories,
latent-shock inversion, and measurement-error evaluation. Posterior simulation
then repeats this stack thousands of times. Approximation is attractive, but an
unmeasured approximation can change the objective, the posterior geometry, and
even the numerical support of the model.

This paper develops a residual-surrogate workflow designed around that risk.
The method solves the complete nonlinear equations at designed parameter,
state, and shock inputs, then learns the difference between those solutions and
a parameter-specific first-order reduced-order model (ROM). The ROM supplies a
disciplined local baseline; the network is responsible only for the remaining
nonlinearity. During sampling, the cheap differentiable objective supplies the
Hamiltonian force. A separately declared density is evaluated at each proposal
endpoint. Standard Metropolis correction then preserves that declared density
when the numerical proposal map is reversible and volume preserving.

The preserved Gal\'i pilots establish that this software path composes on the
complete 23-equation nonlinear model while only the estimated parameter vector
is reduced. Across 80 deterministic extended-path targets, the largest recorded
equation residual is $9.92\times10^{-9}$. Python and JAX evaluations of the same
surrogate profile objective differ by at most $1.71\times10^{-13}$ at the
audited calibration. A calibration block shows a 53.9 percent mean reduction in
state error relative to the ROM after per-output shrinkage is fitted on that
same block. The last number is not an untouched test result, the HMC budgets are
not convergence evidence, and no preserved run yet establishes a nonlinear
posterior speed advantage.

The paper makes three contributions. First, it separates three objects often
conflated in surrogate estimation: nonlinear trajectories used as labels, a
surrogate profile objective used for cheap differentiation, and the nonlinear
likelihood required for a structural-posterior claim. Second, it integrates
parameter-dependent DSGE solution, residual learning, bounded-parameter HMC,
and explicit failure handling in a content-addressed workflow. Third, it turns
publication claims into executable gates covering stochastic expectations,
cross-language parity, untouched test accuracy, posterior fidelity, sampler
diagnostics, and full computational accounting.

The paper does not claim that endpoint correction or neural solution methods
are new by themselves. Its narrow claim is that nonlinear DSGE surrogates can
be made auditable enough to determine when they are safe and useful for
estimation. The rest of the paper defines the estimands, describes the numerical
workflow, proves the target-invariance statement used by the sampler, and
reports the preserved engineering evidence before stating the confirmatory
design and stopping rules.

\subsection{Relation to the literature}

The estimation problem sits between nonlinear DSGE solution, neural
approximation, and accelerated MCMC. Extended path provides the nonlinear
trajectory construction \citep{fair1983extended}; stochastic extended path
replaces future-shock means with numerical integration over conditional
expectations \citep{adjemian2025sep}. Likelihood construction for nonlinear
DSGE models remains model- and solution-dependent \citep{kollmann2017tractable},
which is why this paper treats the trajectory solver, latent-shock treatment,
and statistical objective as separate validation targets.

Neural networks have been used to solve high-dimensional dynamic economic
models \citep{maliar2021deep}. Here the network does not replace the economic
model wholesale: it learns the full-minus-first-order discrepancy and is always
benchmarked against the ROM. Surrogate-gradient HMC and neural-gradient HMC
already establish the proposal-side idea \citep{zhang2017surrogate,li2019nnghmc}.
Our focus is the less forgiving DSGE case in which the endpoint density may
contain parameter-dependent solution failures and may differ from the density
used to generate the force. The independent implementation benchmark uses
MacroModelling.jl \citep{kockerols2023macromodelling}, while the JAX path builds
on the differentiable state-space perspective of
\citet{childers2022differentiable}.

\section{Economic model and statistical objects}

Let $\theta\in\Theta$ denote structural parameters, $s_t$ predetermined states,
$x_t$ endogenous variables, and $\varepsilon_t$ structural shocks. Write the
nonlinear equilibrium system as
\begin{equation}
  F(x_{t-1},x_t,\E_t x_{t+1},\varepsilon_t;\theta)=0.
  \label{eq:equilibrium}
\end{equation}
For a given parameter vector and shock design, a nonlinear extended-path solver
produces a trajectory $x^{F}_{1:T}(\theta)$ from the full-order model (FOM). A
first-order solution produces the cheaper trajectory $x^{R}_{1:T}(\theta)$. We
fit a network $r_{\psi}$ to the residual
\begin{equation}
  \Delta x_t(\theta)=x^{F}_t(\theta)-x^{R}_t(\theta),
  \qquad
  \widehat{x}^{F}_t(\theta)=x^{R}_t(\theta)+r_{\psi}(z_t,\theta),
  \label{eq:residual-surrogate}
\end{equation}
where $z_t$ contains the states and shocks available at the prediction point.
Parameter vectors, rather than individual rows, are assigned to data
partitions. This prevents periods from the same parameter-specific trajectory
from appearing on both sides of a split.

The target nonlinear posterior for data $y_{1:T}$ is
\begin{equation}
  \pi_F(\theta\mid y)\propto p(\theta)L_F(y_{1:T}\mid\theta),
  \label{eq:exact-posterior}
\end{equation}
where $L_F$ denotes an independently validated nonlinear likelihood with a
declared treatment of latent shocks and every required change-of-variables
term. The present implementation instead defines a surrogate inversion objective
$\widetilde L_{\psi}$ by replacing nonlinear trajectories with
\eqref{eq:residual-surrogate}, optimizing latent shocks, and evaluating the
shock and measurement penalties at that optimizer. Unless an integration,
Laplace adjustment, or required change-of-variables term is supplied, this is a
profile or pseudo-likelihood objective rather than a marginal likelihood. The
two objects are therefore scientifically distinct. Agreement between Python and
JAX evaluations of $\widetilde L_{\psi}$ is implementation parity; it is not
evidence that $\widetilde L_{\psi}=L_F$.

More explicitly, let $h_{\psi}(s_t,\varepsilon_t,\theta)$ map a state and
structural shock into the observables implied by the ROM plus residual
surrogate. At each date, the implementation computes
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\varepsilon_t(\theta)
  =\arg\min_{\varepsilon}
  \left\{
  \lVert R^{-1/2}[y_t-h_{\psi}(s_t,\varepsilon,\theta)]\rVert_2^2
  +\lVert Q^{-1/2}\varepsilon\rVert_2^2
  \right\},
  \label{eq:shock-profile}
\end{equation}
using a damped Gauss--Newton iteration, and then sums the Gaussian penalties
evaluated at $\widehat\varepsilon_t(\theta)$. Here $R$ and $Q$ are diagonal in
the pilot. The state passed to date $t+1$ follows the configured ROM or
surrogate replay. Equation~\eqref{eq:shock-profile} makes the missing operation
clear: the latent shocks are optimized, not integrated out. It also clarifies
why agreement between two implementations of this calculation is a software
check rather than likelihood validation.

Table~\ref{tab:objects} records the three numerical objects used below. A
trajectory label is not a density, and a profile objective is not a marginal
likelihood. Keeping their names distinct prevents a correct MCMC kernel from
being used to support the wrong inferential claim.

The benchmark is the complete nonlinear New Keynesian system distributed with
the MacroModelling.jl examples \citep{kockerols2023macromodelling} and associated
with \citet{gali2015}. It contains 23 equations, 16 declared parameters, and
three structural shocks. The reduced experiment estimates the Taylor-rule
responses to inflation and output, $\phi_\pi$ and $\phi_y$, while retaining
every model equation. Reducing the parameter dimension makes a numerical
posterior reference feasible; it does not replace the model with the familiar
three-equation linearization.

\subsection{Pilot data and parameter design}

The preserved software fixture contains ten synthetic observations of log
output, annualized inflation, and the annualized nominal interest rate. The
measurement standard deviation is 0.01 for each observable. The three declared
structural shocks are technology, monetary-policy, and preference shocks, with
model innovation scales 0.01, 0.0025, and 0.05. These observations are useful
for testing the inversion and sampling stack; they are not an empirical data
application. The confirmatory experiment freezes a documented data-generating
parameter, simulation seed, burn-in, initial condition, and shock path before
training or posterior evaluation.

The deterministic default pilot contains eight parameter vectors and ten
periods per vector. The reference calibration is included, and the two estimated
policy coefficients are perturbed by the fixed design while the remaining 14
parameters stay at their declared calibration. Two complete parameter groups
form the calibration block. The positive-order pilot uses the same reduced
parameter design so that its purpose remains solver feasibility rather than a
claim about broad parameter-space coverage.

\section{Residual-surrogate construction}

For training case $i$, let $u_i=(z_i,\theta_i)$ and
$d_i=x^F_i-x^R_i$. Input and output location and scale are estimated only from
the training partition. In standardized coordinates, the implemented data-fit
loss is
\begin{equation}
  \widehat\psi
  =\arg\min_{\psi}
  \frac{1}{\sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm tr}}w_i}
  \sum_{i\in\mathcal I_{\rm tr}}
  w_i\left\|\widetilde d_i-r_\psi(\widetilde u_i)\right\|_2^2,
  \label{eq:training-loss}
\end{equation}
with zero weight for masked failures. The optimizer is AdamW with gradient
clipping. The network embeds states and shocks, conditions hidden activations
on the full estimated parameter vector through feature-wise affine modulation,
and uses residual blocks with SiLU activations. Deployment restores the original
units and adds the predicted discrepancy to the ROM.

The preserved default configuration uses one residual block, 64 hidden units,
400 epochs, batches of 32, and 64-bit training arithmetic. These choices are
engineering settings, not selected hyperparameters. Confirmatory learning curves
vary target count and network capacity using only training and calibration data;
the untouched test partition is evaluated once for the frozen specification.

The implementation can fit an additional per-output shrinkage coefficient in
$[0,1]$ after network training. In the preserved pilots, those coefficients
were fitted on the same parameter block used to report the resulting RMSE.
That block is therefore a calibration set, not an untouched test set. We retain
the values as engineering diagnostics but do not treat them as out-of-sample
evidence. Publication launchers disable this calibration; a future use of
shrinkage requires a distinct calibration partition and an untouched test
partition.

\subsection{Offline and online computations}

The offline stage draws complete parameter vectors, solves a parameter-specific
steady state and first-order ROM, and generates nonlinear extended-path targets
conditional on designed state and shock paths. Each attempted target retains a
case identifier, solver status, residual norm, and parameter-group identifier.
Only the training partition determines feature scaling and network weights.
Validation partitions are disjoint in complete parameter vectors, not merely in
individual rows. The residual network and every preprocessing constant are
then frozen and serialized with the model identity and source hashes.

The online stage transforms an unconstrained vector $q$ to bounded structural
parameters, adds the transform Jacobian to the declared log target, rebuilds
the parameter-dependent objects required by the selected runtime, and evaluates
either the profile objective or a nonlinear reference. A surrogate-gradient
trajectory costs many cheap force evaluations but only one new target
evaluation per transition. This accounting---rather than draws per second
alone---is central when the endpoint target contains a nonlinear solution.

Extended path traces back to \citet{fair1983extended}. Deterministic extended
path sets future innovations to their conditional means and therefore does not
capture Jensen effects in nonlinear expectations. Stochastic extended path
approximates those conditional expectations over future shocks
\citep{adjemian2025sep}. This is why positive branching order, multiple nodes,
and active variation in every shock are hard requirements for confirmatory
evidence, even though order zero is useful for debugging deterministic
nonlinearity.

\section{Target-corrected surrogate trajectories}

Let $q$ be an unconstrained parameterization of $\theta$, let $p$ be Gaussian
momentum with kinetic energy $K(p)$, and let $\pi_T(q)$ be the density the
sampler is intended to preserve. A cheaper differentiable density $\pi_P(q)$
defines the force used in $L$ leapfrog steps. Denote the resulting deterministic
map by $(q',p')=\Phi_{\pi_P}(q,p)$. The acceptance probability is
\begin{equation}
  \alpha\{(q,p),(q',p')\}
  =\min\left\{1,
  \exp\left[-H_T(q',p')+H_T(q,p)\right]\right\},
  \quad
  H_T(q,p)=-\log\pi_T(q)+K(p).
  \label{eq:acceptance}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[Target invariance]
Suppose $\Phi_{\pi_P}$ is reversible after momentum negation and volume
preserving, and $\pi_T$ is evaluated at both endpoints used in
\eqref{eq:acceptance}. Then the Metropolis transition leaves $\pi_T$ invariant,
even when $\pi_P\neq\pi_T$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
On the extended state $z=(q,p)$, the target density is proportional to
$\exp\{-H_T(z)\}$. Compose the leapfrog map with momentum negation. By the
assumptions, the resulting map is an involution with unit Jacobian determinant.
The deterministic Metropolis ratio is therefore
$\exp\{-H_T(z')+H_T(z)\}$, and \eqref{eq:acceptance} gives detailed balance on
the extended state. Marginalizing the refreshed momentum leaves $\pi_T(q)$
invariant. The construction of $\Phi_{\pi_P}$ affects acceptance but not this
invariance argument.
\end{proof}

One transition has four auditable steps. Draw fresh Gaussian momentum; apply
$L$ leapfrog steps using only $\nabla\log\pi_P$; reject immediately if the
proposal or target is non-finite; otherwise apply \eqref{eq:acceptance} using
$\pi_T$ at the two endpoints. Step size and mass-matrix adaptation end with
warmup. The frozen network, normalization constants, parameter transform, and
target implementation are part of the recorded sampling state.

The proposition is the standard deterministic-proposal Metropolis argument for
leapfrog HMC \citep{neal2011hmc}. It places two practical
restrictions on the implementation. First, the surrogate and every preprocessing
transformation used in the force must be frozen during production sampling.
Second, ``exact'' always modifies the declared target. If
$\pi_T\propto p(\theta)\widetilde L_{\psi}$, the chain is exact for the
surrogate-defined posterior, not for \eqref{eq:exact-posterior}. Exact nonlinear
posterior claims require endpoint evaluation of $L_F$ itself.

For bounded parameters $\theta_j\in(a_j,b_j)$, the implementation samples
$q_j\in\mathbb{R}$ and uses
\begin{equation}
  \theta_j=a_j+(b_j-a_j)\operatorname{logit}^{-1}(q_j),
  \qquad
  \log J(q)=\sum_j\left[
    \log(b_j-a_j)+\log\sigma(q_j)+\log\{1-\sigma(q_j)\}
  \right].
  \label{eq:bounded-transform}
\end{equation}
Thus the endpoint target is defined in $q$ coordinates and must include
$\log J(q)$. Invalid steady states, indeterminate local solutions, failed
nonlinear paths, or non-finite objective values must be rejected as points
outside support. A large finite penalty is not equivalent: it creates an
artificial plateau with positive density. The preserved pilot still uses such a
sentinel and therefore cannot support an exact structural-posterior claim.

The sampler has three implementation-level regression checks. When proposal and
target densities coincide, its draws and accept/reject decisions match the
ordinary static-HMC implementation under the same key. Under a deliberately
shifted and rescaled proposal force, a fixed-seed multi-chain test recovers the
known moments of a standard-normal target after endpoint correction. Finally,
non-finite candidate targets receive zero acceptance probability. These tests
check kernel plumbing; they do not replace convergence and posterior-fidelity
evidence for the DSGE application.

This distinction is especially relevant for differentiable state-space and
DSGE estimation, where automatic differentiation improves computation but does
not resolve approximation error in the target \citep{childers2022differentiable}.
Related work has made nonlinear DSGE likelihoods more tractable
\citep{kollmann2017tractable}; our evaluation therefore treats accuracy against
an independently computed nonlinear reference as a primary outcome.

\section{Validation design}

The confirmatory design has two tiers. Tier 1 estimates $(\phi_\pi,\phi_y)$ and
uses a dense posterior grid. Grid resolution is doubled until posterior moments
and interval endpoints stabilize. This tier isolates numerical correctness from
high-dimensional MCMC behavior. Tier 2 estimates a prespecified four-to-six
parameter subset and measures scaling with four independently initialized
chains. The paper will report Tier 2 only after Tier 1 passes.

Training, validation, and posterior-region designs are disjoint in parameter
vectors, shock sequences, and trajectories. Validation includes regular
prior-interior points, posterior-region points, and a boundary-stress stratum.
The last stratum is reported separately rather than deleted through a stability-
margin screen. For stochastic extended path, evidence runs use positive
branching order, multiple quadrature nodes, and nonzero coverage of every
declared shock. Common case identifiers are evaluated by both the Python and
MacroModelling.jl implementations.

For trajectory case $i$ with $d_x$ reported outputs, the primary approximation
metric is the scale-adjusted root mean squared error
\begin{equation}
 e^{x}_i=\left\{\frac{1}{Td_x}
 \sum_{t=1}^{T}\left\|D_x^{-1}
 (\widehat x^F_{it}-x^F_{it})\right\|_2^2\right\}^{1/2},
 \label{eq:trajectory-error}
\end{equation}
where $D_x$ is fixed from the training partition. Paired differences
$e^x_{i,\mathrm{ROM}}-e^x_{i,\mathrm{sur}}$ are summarized with a 95 percent
interval over prespecified test cases. Reporting includes medians, upper-tail
quantiles, and every output separately; an average cannot hide failure in one
observable.

Likelihood geometry is compared after removing an arbitrary additive constant.
For reference point $\theta_0$, define
\begin{equation}
 e^\ell(\theta)=
 \bigl[\log\widetilde L_\psi(\theta)-
       \log\widetilde L_\psi(\theta_0)\bigr]
 -\bigl[\log L_F(\theta)-\log L_F(\theta_0)\bigr].
 \label{eq:centered-likelihood-error}
\end{equation}
The grid analysis reports the distribution of $e^\ell$, rank reversals near the
mode, and normalized gradient discrepancies only where the reference gradient
exceeds a frozen floor. Posterior means and interval endpoints are compared to
their combined grid-discretization or Monte Carlo uncertainty, not to an
arbitrary number of printed decimal places.

The primary accuracy outcomes are scaled equilibrium residuals; held-out,
per-output trajectory errors; paired improvement over the ROM with uncertainty;
centered log-likelihood error; rank reversals near the mode; and normalized
gradient error away from zero-gradient cases. Posterior comparison reports
differences relative to pooled Monte Carlo error, interval endpoint differences,
and a distributional diagnostic. Sampling reports rank-normalized split
$\widehat R$, bulk and tail effective sample sizes (ESS), ESS per gradient
evaluation, and ESS per second.

Timing uses the same workload, precision, and hardware for both methods.
Compilation, host callbacks, device transfers, and training are reported
separately. Warm likelihood microbenchmarks use repeated synchronized calls;
end-to-end comparisons use independent processes. The current Schur QME path
invokes a sequential SciPy host callback, so a GPU label does not imply a
GPU-native likelihood. The available GPU-native matrix-sign alternative must
pass Gal\'i-specific solution and likelihood parity before it enters a speed
claim.

The primary decision is sequential. Experiment A first asks whether the
two-parameter nonlinear reference and surrogate conclusions agree within the
frozen numerical tolerances. A failure stops escalation to the higher-dimensional
sampling experiment. Conditional on correctness, Experiment B asks whether the
method improves effective samples per second after offline target generation,
training, compilation, transfers, and failed solves are included. A method can
therefore pass the correctness gate and fail the computational-value gate.

The protocol is frozen before confirmatory execution. It records the repository
revision, dirty-worktree state, environment, command, seeds, hardware, precision,
and content hashes for every evidence artifact. Numerical failures remain in
attempted-case denominators and are stratified by prior-interior,
posterior-region, and boundary-stress designs. Machine-readable evidence is the
source of manuscript tables; hand-entered numerical cells are not permitted.

The pilot establishes three narrow facts. The parser, parameter-specific

convergence evidence and are not a speed advantage. The acceptance rate instead
suggests that the static-HMC tuning is far from an efficient production regime.

\section{Limitations and interpretation}

The preserved evidence is an engineering pilot. Its nonlinear targets use
branching order zero and one node, so it does not validate stochastic
expectations. Most likelihood calls hold the reference steady state and ROM
fixed. Its reported surrogate RMSE is calibration-adjusted rather than measured
on an untouched test partition. The parameter-dependent full-JAX run still targets the surrogate profile
inversion objective, assigns a finite $-10^{12}$ sentinel to failed solves, and
uses a host-callback Schur solve. An exact structural posterior instead requires
explicit support handling and an economically defined likelihood. The sampler
budgets are too small for convergence diagnostics, the data are synthetic and short,
and no independent Julia trajectory or likelihood comparison is yet archived.
The current evidence therefore supports neither exact nonlinear posterior
sampling nor an end-to-end speedup claim.

These limitations determine the paper's next experiments rather than merely
qualifying its conclusion. A credible methods result requires the two-parameter
reference experiment to pass before scaling the posterior dimension. If the
surrogate does not preserve the grid posterior, or if its full accounting does
not improve ESS per second, the negative result should be reported and the claim
reduced to numerical infrastructure.

\section{Conclusion}

Residual learning may be useful for nonlinear DSGE estimation because a
first-order model provides a disciplined baseline and the network concentrates
on the nonlinear discrepancy. Target-corrected HMC can remove error from the
trajectory force, but only relative to the density actually evaluated at the
Metropolis endpoints. The preserved Gal\'i pilot shows that the software path is
feasible and reveals where apparently favorable diagnostics can overstate the
evidence. Publication readiness depends on stochastic-SEP validation, an
untouched test design, an independently converged nonlinear posterior reference,
cross-language parity, efficient multi-chain sampling, and same-work timing.
The accompanying protocol makes those requirements falsifiable and preserves
negative results rather than redefining success after a run.
